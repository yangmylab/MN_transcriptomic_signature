**Re: submission of an original research article — "A compartment-resolved glomerular transcriptomic
signature and a four-gene classifier for membranous nephropathy across three independent cohorts"**

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as an original research article in *EBioMedicine*.
The study asks a narrow, clinically motivated question: does the kidney transcriptome of membranous
nephropathy (MN) contain a compact signature that separates it from the proteinuric glomerulopathies
it mimics, and does that signature survive independent validation?

**What we found.** We harmonised 13 public GEO accessions (2,165 samples; 453 MN samples from 178
unique patients) and resolved patient-level redundancy before any modelling, which left seven
non-redundant cohort sets and three genuinely independent sample sources: NEPTUNE microarrays for
discovery, the European Renal cDNA Bank for external validation with no shared participants, and two
RNA-seq cohorts for cross-platform replication. Thresholded differential expression is thin against
FSGS (18 genes), yet a classifier learns the difference (nested-CV AUC 0.91–0.93; external ERCB AUC
0.86–0.92 after each validation cohort is harmonised to the discovery reference distribution and
scored with training coefficients only). The signal is
confined to two small, ERCB-preserved glomerular co-expression modules — a podocyte surface/calcium
programme and a lysosomal/glycosylation programme — both of which survive adjustment for cell
composition. Four genes (*SLAMF8*, *TRPC6*, *MYOZ2*, *PPP1R3C*) approach a 13,379-gene model's external
discrimination: AUC 0.816 (95 % CI 0.721–0.910) in ERCB versus 0.922 for the whole-transcriptome
model, 0.901 in the RNA-seq cohort, 0.929 in an
independent laser-capture cohort and 0.848 across a different array annotation. The panel is
compartment-specific (chance in the tubulointerstitium of the same patients, 0.45/0.54) and requires
per-platform recalibration, which we demonstrate; both PLA2R strata are detected against MCD
(0.98/0.88), while the within-MN contrast (6 vs 6) is reported as uninterpretable.

**Why this journal.** The paper is a translational bridge rather than a bioinformatics exercise: it
takes a clinically unresolved question (whether tissue transcriptomics adds anything to biopsy and
serology), answers it with externally validated discrimination, localises the signal to cells using a
236,107-cell kidney atlas, and states plainly where it stops — a discrimination instrument, not a
calibrated risk calculator. We also report two negative results in full (exploratory subtyping does
not reproduce; the tubulointerstitial compartment carries no MN-specific signal) and a
non-significant refit-based permutation null (*p* = 0.11), because they constrain how the panel should
be used.
